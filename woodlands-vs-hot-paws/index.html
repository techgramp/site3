Woodlands vs Hot Paws Pet Resort: Worth Crossing the State Line?

St. Marys private suites compared with an Amelia Island resort

Home / Compare / Woodlands vs Hot Paws

Hot Paws Pet Resort on Amelia Island comes up often when St. Marys owners ask about boarding, partly because plenty of people here work or shop across the state line anyway. It is a locally owned, family‑operated resort offering boarding, day camp and grooming, and by their own account they have been at it for years in Fernandina Beach.

Woodlands is a small boarding facility in St. Marys itself, with private suites and no group play between unrelated dogs. The two genuinely differ on three things: how far you have to drive, whether dogs play in groups, and what is published about pricing. This page covers all three honestly.

What we could verify about Hot Paws pricing

A third‑party pet‑hotel directory lists Hot Paws as starting around $30 per night, but that figure is not published on their own site, carries no date, and does not specify room type or what is included. We are flagging it rather than presenting it as their price. For an accurate quote, call Hot Paws on (904) 277‑3075 or use the booking system on their website.

Two things worth asking them about, because they materially affect the total: whether there is a deposit, and whether holiday or peak periods carry a surcharge. Woodlands has none of either.

The drive is the first real decision

Hot Paws is on South 8th Street in Fernandina Beach, which from central St. Marys means crossing into Florida, typically 30 to 40 minutes each way depending on traffic over the bridges. For a two‑week vacation that is one round trip out and one back, and barely matters. For a long weekend, or for regular day camp, it is a different calculation entirely.

If you are already heading to Amelia Island regularly, that drive is close to free and their location is genuinely convenient. If your dog needs day care during a work week, Woodlands being in St. Marys with three daily drop‑off windows is likely the more practical option.

Grouped play versus one family at a time

Hot Paws describes something we think is a genuine strength of their model: a daycare coordinator who assesses each dog and places them with playmates of similar size and temperament, in a large backyard. That is a thoughtful way to run group play, and for a social dog it produces the kind of tired, happy dog that solo care simply cannot.

Woodlands does not do that at all. Dogs are grouped only with their own household, and never with dogs from other families. Outdoor time and play happen with their own siblings or with us. This exists for the dogs that group play does not suit: the reactive ones, the anxious ones, the seniors who would rather not be bounced on, and the ones whose owners simply do not want to find out the hard way.

Neither is better in the abstract. Be honest about which dog you have.

Who may prefer Woodlands

  • Owners who would rather not drive into Florida and back
  • Dogs who do better without group play
  • Anyone who wants rates published up front
  • Owners who want no deposit and no cancellation penalty
  • People who want a person on site overnight
  • Regular day‑stay users for whom the drive would repeat weekly

Who may prefer Hot Paws

  • Dogs who love supervised group play with matched playmates
  • Owners already travelling to Amelia Island regularly
  • Families needing cat boarding as well
  • Anyone who wants boarding, day camp and grooming in one building
  • Owners who value their longer track record in the area
  • Anyone needing dates we cannot cover, since we are small

Hot Paws may be the better fit if their group‑care model, island location, combined services or availability suits you more closely.

Questions worth asking any boarding facility

Where does my dog sleep?A private suite, a shared run, or an open room?
Are unrelated dogs mixed together?Is group play the default, and can you opt out?
Who is present overnight?Someone on site, someone on call, or cameras only?
Is there a deposit or cancellation fee?And does it change around holidays?
Are there peak‑season surcharges?Holiday pricing can change the total significantly.
How are feeding instructions handled?Can you send your own food and keep their schedule?
Can medications be given?Is there a fee, and who administers them?
What happens in an emergency?Which vet, and how fast are you contacted?
Can I tour before booking?Any confident facility will say yes.
How are anxious or senior dogs handled?Is the routine adjusted per dog?
What vaccinations are required?Requirements protect every dog there.
Is spay/neuter required?Some group facilities restrict intact adult dogs.
