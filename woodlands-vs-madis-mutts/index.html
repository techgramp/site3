Woodlands Pet Retreat vs Madi's Mutts: Which Is Right for Your Dog?

Comparing private‑suite boarding with a group daycare model in St. Marys

Home / Compare / Woodlands vs Madi's Mutts

Choosing where your dog stays is a personal decision, and St. Marys is lucky to have more than one genuine option. Woodlands Pet Retreat and Madi's Mutts both board dogs here in town, but they are built around different ideas about what a good day looks like for a dog. This guide lays out what we can verify about both, so you can decide which model suits your dog rather than taking anyone's word for it.

The short version: Madi's Mutts is a daycare‑forward facility where supervised group play and socialisation are central, and they also offer boarding and grooming on site. Woodlands is a small boarding facility where dogs stay in private suites and are never grouped with dogs from other households. Both are family run. Neither approach is universally better, and the right answer depends almost entirely on your particular dog.

The main difference: group play or one family at a time

This is the distinction that matters most between the two, and it is worth understanding properly before you book anywhere.

Madi's Mutts describes supervised indoor and outdoor play with socialisation as a core part of what they do, including a treated turf yard. For a confident, social, high‑energy dog who genuinely enjoys other dogs, that kind of day is excellent. Plenty of dogs come home from group daycare happily exhausted in a way no amount of solo attention achieves.

Woodlands is built the other way round. This is a one‑on‑one structured facility: dogs are grouped only with their own family, and dogs from different households are never mixed together. Play and outdoor time happen with their own people and their own siblings. That suits dogs who find other dogs stressful rather than fun, dogs who are reactive on lead, seniors who would rather not be bounced on, and dogs whose owners simply do not want to gamble on group dynamics.

If your dog loves a crowd, the group model is a real advantage and you should weigh it seriously. If your dog does not, our model exists precisely for that.

Where the two are more similar than you might expect

Both are family run rather than chain operations, which usually means you talk to the person responsible for your dog rather than a call centre. Both describe monitoring systems in their buildings. Both offer boarding, daytime care and access to grooming, though we handle grooming through our sister salon, Chien d'amour, rather than in the same building.

One factual difference worth asking about directly: their published information describes being on site daily from 7am to 9pm year‑round, while at Woodlands a caretaker is on site overnight as well. If overnight presence matters to you, ask both of us to explain exactly what happens between midnight and 6am, and compare the answers.

Questions worth asking any boarding facility

These are the questions that actually reveal how a facility operates. Ask us, and ask anywhere else you are considering.

Where does my dog sleep?A private suite, a shared run, or an open room with other dogs?
How often do dogs go outside?Scheduled breaks, or only when staff have time?
Are unrelated dogs mixed together?Is group play the default, and can you opt out?
Who is present overnight?A person on site, a person on call, or cameras only?
How are dogs monitored?Cameras, in‑person checks, or both?
How are feeding instructions handled?Can you send your own food and keep their schedule?
Can medications be given?Is there a fee, and who administers them?
What happens in an emergency?Which vet, and how quickly are you contacted?
Can I tour before booking?Any facility confident in its space will say yes.
What vaccinations are required?Requirements protect every dog staying there.
What should I bring?Bedding, toys and food from home help dogs settle.
How are anxious or senior dogs handled?Is the routine adjusted, or is it the same for every dog?
