Local information from Woodlands Pet Retreat

The St. Marys Welcome Center is a helpful starting point for a first visit or a return trip to coastal Georgia. Located at 400 Osborne Street in the historic district, it provides visitor information, maps, sightseeing ideas and resources for discovering the town and planning time on Cumberland Island.

At Woodlands Pet Retreat, we value the local organizations that help people experience St. Marys. This independent guide is our way of directing travelers to a useful local resource while also answering a common question: what should I do with my dog while I explore?

Plan your visit

Why start at the Welcome Center?

Explore Georgia describes the Welcome Center as a source for information about local lodging, restaurants, shops, museums and adventures. Visitors can learn about the historic district, pick up a self-guided walking tour brochure, and ask about local tour options.

The center also highlights local history with exhibits including the Georgia Radio Museum and Hall of Fame and War of 1812-related material. It is a useful stop before deciding how to spend your time by the waterfront.

Traveling with a dog?

Enjoy the town. We'll help plan their stay.

St. Marys has outdoor places to visit with your dog, but not every tour, building or ferry ride accommodates pets. If your itinerary includes a museum, group tour or Cumberland Island outing, arrange appropriate care rather than leaving your dog unattended in a vehicle.

Woodlands offers private-suite overnight dog boarding and day stays from our St. Marys location. Review hours and availability before committing to a particular tour or ferry departure.

Questions, answered

Frequently asked questions

Where is the St. Marys Welcome Center?

It is located at 400 Osborne Street in the historic district of St. Marys, Georgia.

Is the Welcome Center free to visit?

Explore Georgia lists free admission. Check the official listing for any updated hours, exhibits or tour details.

Can I take my dog on the Cumberland Island passenger ferry?

No. The National Park Service says pets are not transported on the concession-operated ferry. Qualified service animals have separate rules.
