Going to Cumberland Island?
Your dog cannot come.

The National Park Service ferry does not carry pets. We take day guests while you are on the island.

Day stays from $25 Private suite, outdoor breaks, home the same evening

People arrive in St. Marys all the time having planned a Cumberland Island day trip, and only then find out that the ferry will not take their dog. It is one of the most common surprises visitors run into here. The good news is that it is easy to solve, and you do not have to cancel your trip.

Woodlands Pet Retreat is a small boarding facility a few minutes from the ferry dock. We take day guests for $25 a day, in a private indoor suite with outdoor breaks on a quiet wooded lakefront. Drop off before the morning sailing, collect them when you are back on the mainland.

The rule, plainly

The National Park Service ferry between St. Marys and Cumberland Island transports passengers only. Pets are not permitted on the ferry, in campgrounds, or inside buildings. Service animals are the exception. Dogs can reach the island by private boat, and are then allowed on roads and trails on the south end on a six‑foot leash, but not in the wilderness area that covers the northern half.

Ferry schedules, fees and rules change by season. Confirm the current details with the National Park Service before you travel, and with the St. Marys Welcome Center below.

If the weather turns

Ferries on this coast get cancelled, and returns get delayed. If that happens, call us on (912) 674-4709. We would much rather roll a day stay into an overnight than have you racing the clock. Overnight boarding starts at $45 a night, and there is no deposit and no cancellation fee either way.

What to bring

  • Their food, measured for the day, plus a bowl if you have one
  • Current rabies, bordetella and DHLPP records, which your vet can email us directly
  • Any medication, with written instructions
  • A favourite toy or blanket if it helps them settle

Start at the St. Marys Welcome Center

If you are planning a trip here, the St. Marys Welcome Center is the official source of travel information for both St. Marys and Cumberland Island. They are at 400 Osborne Street in the historic district, open Monday to Saturday 9:00 AM to 5:00 PM and Sunday 12:00 to 5:00 PM, with free admission and free parking.

They carry brochures on lodging, dining and the island, run a 15‑minute film about St. Marys, offer self‑guided tour brochures and a one‑hour golf‑cart tram tour of the historic district, and house the Georgia Radio Museum and Hall of Fame, a War of 1812 banner display and the Georgia Coast Naturescape. If you have questions about ferry timing or what else to do while you are here, they are the people to ask.

We are grateful to the Welcome Center team for pointing visitors our way when they arrive with a dog and ferry tickets. Woodlands Pet Retreat is an independent business and is not affiliated with the Welcome Center, Explore Georgia or the National Park Service.

Common questions

Can I take my dog on the Cumberland Island ferry?

No. The National Park Service ferry from St. Marys does not transport pets. Only service animals are permitted on the ferry, in campgrounds and inside buildings. Dogs can reach the island by private boat, but not on the passenger ferry.

What do I do with my dog while I visit Cumberland Island?

Book a day stay. Woodlands Pet Retreat is a few minutes from the ferry dock in St. Marys and takes day guests for $25 a day, in a private indoor suite with outdoor breaks through the day.

Will the drop-off times work with the ferry schedule?

Yes. Our morning window is 6:30 to 8:00 AM, which is before the first sailing, and our evening window is 4:30 to 6:00 PM, which covers the afternoon returns. Tell us which ferry you are on and we will make sure the timing works.

What if the ferry is cancelled or I miss the last boat?

Call us. Weather cancellations happen on this coast and we would far rather convert your day stay into an overnight than leave you stuck. Overnight boarding starts at $45.

Are dogs allowed anywhere on Cumberland Island?

Dogs that arrive by private boat are allowed on roads and trails on the south end of the island on a six-foot leash, but not in the wilderness area covering the north half, not in campgrounds and not inside buildings. Confirm current rules with the National Park Service before you plan around it.

What vaccinations do you need for a day stay?

Rabies, bordetella and DHLPP must be current. Your vet can email records to us directly, which is usually the quickest route if you are travelling.
